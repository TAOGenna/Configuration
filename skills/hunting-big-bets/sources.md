# Sources and distillation

## Felix Rieseberg

**[Hunger beats tenure](https://felixrieseberg.com/hunger-beats-tenure-advice-for-junior-engineers/)** (Oct 2026)
- Execution isn't expensive anymore, so the org chart no longer gates ideas. A working prototype does.
- Fresh eyes are an asset ("maintain an influx of the naive and ignorant", March 1991). Notion's "impossible" offline mode turned out to rest on founder resistance that nobody had checked. Within a year, anyone can become the old-timer who says "I already tried".
- What to do: (1) pick business impact over the backlog, and aim at your manager's manager's goals; (2) pick a radical idea; (3) build it over a weekend instead of writing a PRD and lobbying for buy-in; (4) put one idea per prototype, so leadership sees the opportunity *and* that you can run with it; (5) lean on your fresh perspective, either as user empathy or as ignorance of what is "impossible".

**[How to be more ambitious](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/)** (Oct 2026)
- **Altitude:** ask for the outcome, not the task, and for the goal beyond the goal ("keep the app crash-free" instead of "fix this crash").
- **Depth:** use the instruments the best practitioners use, start from raw data, pick unreasonable targets, challenge what you believe against raw data, and use an outside judge, never the model judging itself.
- **Range:** turn nouns into verbs (a QBR is really "getting busy people to believe a cause-effect story and decide in 30 minutes"), then assemble the crew of fields that does that verb. Have the model *do* what each expert does; don't role-play them.

## geohot ([blog](https://geohot.github.io/blog/))

- **Play the right game; the consensus can be wrong.** For comma to win, all it took was 2016's consensus being wrong about LIDAR, mapping and hand coding. Most competitors "weren't even playing the right game" and died with billions of dollars. comma is profitable after 10 years. ([Can tinygrad win?](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2025/07/06/can-tinygrad-win.html))
- **Software before the chip.** Ten or so companies taped out chips, which is the easy, capital-heavy part, and none wrote usable software. "Only a fool begins by taping out a chip." ([tiny corp raised $5.1M](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2023/05/24/the-tiny-corp-raised-5M.html), [Five years of tinygrad](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2025/12/29/five-years-of-tinygrad.html))
- **The best part is no part.** About 98% of code works around other code. Each piece "isn't focused on the goal, it's focused on the other pieces", and organizations have the same dysfunction. tinygrad is 1000x smaller than the stack it replaces. (Five years of tinygrad)
- **The bitter lesson.** There should be no "cone guy" in a driving codebase: learn from data, and use search and learning. Draw the line only where a working example exists to copy. ([Where the Bitter Lesson ends](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2024/03/27/where-the-bitter-lesson-ends.html))
- **Money is the map, not the territory.** Create value rather than capture it. Rent-seeking, and jobs that "create complexity for others", get found out. "Go create value for others and don't worry about the returns." ([Money is the Map](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2025/02/24/money-is-the-map.html), [69 agents](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2026/03/11/running-69-agents.html), [Changing the World](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2026/03/23/changing-the-world.html))
- **The builder test.** If the money and the users went away, would you keep building it? comma's real goal is "one perfect box that drives a car better than a human". ([anticloud hopecore](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2025/10/17/anticloud-hopecore.html))
- **Give users the thing, not revocable access to it.** A subscription that can be revoked is feudalism. Ask whether it centralizes or decentralizes power. Hostility toward users drives them to competitors. ([OpenAI is nothing without its people](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2026/04/11/openai-people.html), [Technology without Industry](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2021/01/18/technology-without-industry.html), [Anthropic is making a huge mistake](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2026/01/15/anthropic-huge-mistake.html))
- **Polish over options.** Sell one configuration, give it a simple name, and add one to the number each year. "comma has been around for ten years following this playbook." Test every unit in a harsher environment than it will see in use. ([Bikeshedding](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2025/11/29/bikeshedding-or-laptop.html), [Dyson CameraJet](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2026/10/03/dyson-camerajet.html))
- **Committees can't say yes.** Intel had "nobody who can say yes, just many people who can say no"; Lisa Su answered his first email. Pitch to a person, not a process. ([Tragic Intel](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2025/03/24/tragic-intel.html))
- **Secrecy is a signal.** A "contact us" form gating sales, or a demo only under NDA, usually means the product isn't competitive. ([AI chip companies](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2021/06/13/a-breakdown-of-ai-chip-companies.html))
- **Truth over hype.** "There's a much bigger market for truths that pump bags." AI is a tool, like a compiler, and the value you get from it is search and optimization, not magic. ([AI Coding](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2025/09/12/ai-coding.html), [I love LLMs, I hate hype](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2026/07/12/i-love-llms.html))
- **Staged, self-funding bets.** Sell computers for more than they cost to make. Cherry Computer was planned as FPGA (raise nothing) → $5M tapeout → serious chip, with each stage paying for itself before the next one. ([AI chip companies](https://geohot.github.io/blog/jekyll/update/2021/06/13/a-breakdown-of-ai-chip-companies.html))

## George Hotz — "Outwit, Outplay, Outlast" (COMMA_CON 2025)

[Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=06uotu7aKug). A local transcript, if you saved one, is at `~/.claude/hunting-big-bets/talk-transcript.txt`.

- **There is no Mr. Ford.** The original plan was to sell to carmakers. "There is no Mr. Ford", meaning no one at the company could say yes. Selling to carmakers is "fake legitimacy, a figurehead polishing machine". comma pivoted to asking "can a cell phone drive a car?" and shipped the comma one in about 6 months.
- **Dunning-Kruger is the fuel.** "We didn't do it because it was easy. We did it because we thought it was going to be easy." It took four years to ship the next "phone in a 3D-printed case".
- **"When they go left, eat a cheesecake."** In 2016 the media declared comma dead. Gell-Mann amnesia: don't let outside narratives decide your bet.
- **Price is part of the product.** comma 3 was "too expensive and undifferentiated", lost $5M, and sales stagnated. The 3X "sold for a good price and that fixed sales". The comma body goes from $1,000 to $200 because "it's a toy, price it like a toy". A $1,000 comma 4 that you use on 40% of days is worth it.
- **The test for real technology.** Can you describe it to a six-year-old, and can I get one and use it? "It's not real technology if you see a video." Waymo counts, despite teleop and doubtful unit economics, because you can download the app and ride. The disconnect between hype and reality is huge: "they're better at hype; comma is better at making a product".
- **Success in an infinite game is survival.** Chess is a finite game; a company is not. Ghost ($220M), Embark (SPAC), Argo ($2.7B hit to Ford) and Cruise ($10B) all died, and Cruise had first tried an aftermarket kit and quit because it was too hard. "Waymo outlasted the competition."
- **Measure the gap to the one real competitor in orders of magnitude.** comma vs Tesla: fleet 3 orders of magnitude, training compute 2, inference compute 2, yearly spend 4. Close gaps cheaply: building their own computer factory took training from 3 orders to 2; plugging in a $300 consumer GPU took inference from 2 orders to 0.
- **Ask a different question from the leader.** Tesla asks "how can I ship this crazy feature?"; comma asks "how do we achieve highway perfection?" and wants to "make driving chill".
- **Predict with trend lines.** Every order of magnitude takes the same time. Human drivers crash about every 500k miles; FSD is at about 3k miles per critical disengagement and gets 2x better each year, which gives about 8 years for Tesla and 10 for comma. Both the hype camp and the "never" camp are wrong. "Having accurate predictions about technology is super important if you want to know where to invest."
- **Build the self-driving, not the car.** "A self-driving car is not a car. It's a dude." The ultimate comma is a robot that gets into the driver's seat, which sidesteps carmaker security and gatekeeping. But "don't do this tomorrow, it won't work": timing comes from the trend.
- **Zero extra risk.** "The question is not whether you want to shorten the gap, it's how much extra risk you're willing to take, and the answer is zero." Being two years behind Tesla is fine if the business sustains itself. Tesla pays $200–500M for a cluster where comma pays $2M, a premium to be early that isn't worth it.
- **Moonshots are for the profitable.** Optimus is a good bet for Tesla: about 0.1% of the budget for PR that's useful today. For a startup to raise big money on humanoid robots is "absurd".
- **Use the simplest form that pays.** Vacuums are the first robot form factor that pays for itself. For security, "95% of what you want is a fixed-mount camera, a loudspeaker and good software", so you don't need the robot.
- **Don't ask to be put to work.** "How much would you pay me?" "Negative." Asking adds managerial overhead. Do the bounties: bring the work already done.
- **Never switch to exploiting.** "Innovation's over, let's exploit, crank up the subscriptions, put ads in" is mocked. There is nothing worth buying with that money.
- **Simplicity is reliability.** tinygrad is about 100x simpler than the stack it replaces, and that simplicity is the path to deterministic, reliable robots.
